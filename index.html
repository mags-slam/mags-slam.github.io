TL;DR: The first RGB-only Multi-Agent 3D Gaussian Splatting SLAM for collaborative photorealistic scene reconstruction.

Abstract

  • Problem. Existing multi-agent Gaussian SLAM methods rely on RGB-D sensors for metric depth and cross-agent alignment, which limits their deployment on low-cost or power-constrained robots.
  • Method. Each agent builds local monocular Gaussian submaps and sends compact submap summaries. Geometry- and appearance-aware loop verification and occupancy-aware Gaussian fusion then merge them into one global map despite monocular scale ambiguity.
  • Benchmark. We introduce ReplicaMultiAgent Plus, which contains larger robot teams for evaluating collaborative Gaussian SLAM.
  • Result. On synthetic and real-world datasets, MAGS-SLAM uses RGB images alone, yet its tracking, rendering and geometric quality are competitive with or superior to those of state-of-the-art RGB-D collaborative Gaussian SLAM methods.
Full abstract

Collaborative photorealistic 3D reconstruction from multiple agents enables rapid large-scale scene capture for virtual production and cooperative multi-robot exploration. While recent 3D Gaussian Splatting (3DGS) SLAM algorithms can generate high-fidelity real-time mapping, most of the existing multi-agent Gaussian SLAM methods still rely on RGB-D sensors to obtain metric depth and simplify cross-agent alignment, limiting their deployment on low-cost or power-constrained robotic platforms, especially given the wider availability of RGB cameras.

To address this challenge, we propose MAGS-SLAM, the first RGB-only multi-agent 3DGS SLAM framework for collaborative scene reconstruction. Each agent independently builds local monocular Gaussian submaps and transmits compact submap summaries rather than raw observations. To facilitate robust collaboration in the presence of monocular scale ambiguity, our framework integrates compact submap communication, geometry- and appearance-aware loop verification, and occupancy-aware Gaussian fusion, enabling coherent global reconstruction without active depth sensors.

We further introduce ReplicaMultiAgent Plus, a benchmark containing larger robot teams for evaluating collaborative Gaussian SLAM. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that MAGS-SLAM, using RGB images alone, achieves tracking accuracy, rendering quality, and geometric quality that are competitive with or superior to those of state-of-the-art RGB-D collaborative Gaussian SLAM methods.

Why Multiple Agents

A single agent reconstructs only the part of the scene it has visited. On the real-world 7-Scenes Fire sequence, each agent covers a partial region of the room, shown over a faint outline of the complete scene, while collaborative fusion yields a complete and globally consistent reconstruction. The viewpoint stays fixed when switching, so coverage can be compared directly.

Quantitative Comparison

Using RGB input alone, MAGS-SLAM obtains the lowest average ATE among the RGB-only methods on all four ReplicaMultiAgent scenes (0.29–0.46 cm), while the RGB-D MAGiC-SLAM remains lower at 0.16–0.32 cm. On the real-world AriaMultiagent rooms its average ATE of 0.86 and 0.54 cm is the lowest of all methods, including MAGiC-SLAM at 1.15 and 0.65 cm. Rendering quality ranks first on most scenes: 43.24, 42.83 and 38.99 dB PSNR on Office-0, Apartment-0 and Apartment-2, 29.03 and 31.02 dB on the two Aria rooms, 3.6 and 2.9 dB above the strongest RGB-D competitor, and first place on all five ETH3D scenes with gains of 3–7 dB over MAC-Ego3D.

Scaling to Four Agents

On ReplicaMultiAgent Plus, four independently scaled monocular submaps must be aligned without any depth cue. MAGS-SLAM still renders best on all three scenes, at 40.19, 40.86 and 41.07 dB PSNR on Office-2, Apartment-0 and Apartment-2, against 32.17, 26.53 and 27.50 dB for MAC-Ego3D. Its ATE is the lowest on Office-2 (0.354 cm) and Apartment-0 (0.282 cm), and third on Apartment-2 (0.404 cm) behind MAC-Ego3D and MAGiC-SLAM. MultiSlam-DiffPose, the other RGB method, produces no renderable map.

Ablation

  • Replacing the Sim(3) pose graph with a rigid SE(3) one collapses PSNR to 18.61 dB, so explicit scale estimation is required when every agent is monocular.
  • Naive Gaussian-centre deduplication instead of the occupancy-aware fusion costs 7.2 dB, because slightly misaligned populations from different agents form double shells on shared surfaces.
  • Without the Sim(3)-PGBA update, residual scale drift inside each submap remains and PSNR falls by 4.0 dB.
  • A purely geometric inter-agent residual drops PSNR to 37.88 dB; the photometric term constrains overlaps that are textured but geometrically ambiguous, such as wall–floor seams.
  • Skipping the joint pose–Gaussian refinement costs 1.4 dB, the smallest drop, yet this step supplies the sub-voxel correction that sharp rendering needs.

Efficiency and Scalability

  • Each agent tracks at 13–16 FPS, above the 10 Hz real-time line on all three datasets, with peak GPU memory below 3.9 GB, so several agents fit on one 16 GB card.
  • Retrieval, per-candidate verification and the full Sim(3) pose-graph optimization each finish within the 33 ms budget of a 30 Hz frame, so collaboration does not stall the front-ends.
  • Sending compact submap summaries instead of the raw RGB stream cuts each agent’s uplink by 45–520×.

From two to four agents, per-agent throughput and memory stay flat, and the coordinator cost does not grow: per-candidate verification even falls from 93.6 to 53.4 ms, because a fixed-size scene is covered by shorter per-agent trajectories as more agents join.

Real-World Deployment

Two people each walk the same outdoor residential street with an ordinary smartphone on a handheld monopod, recording monocular RGB video with no depth sensor, stereo rig or calibration between them. The scene has outdoor-scale depth, changing natural light, repetitive facades, parked cars and a passing pedestrian. From the two independently scaled streams, MAGS-SLAM recovers both trajectories and one globally consistent Gaussian map.

ReplicaMultiAgent Plus

A multi-agent benchmark for collaborative Gaussian SLAM, with diverse indoor scenes captured by larger teams whose trajectories overlap. Besides monocular or RGB-D streams and ground-truth poses for each agent, it provides per-frame semantic labels and dense ground-truth meshes, to support research on multi-agent semantic SLAM, open-vocabulary 3D scene understanding and collaborative perception.
